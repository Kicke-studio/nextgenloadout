Standard USB 3.0 hard drives cannot play next-gen games

PS5 and Xbox Series X|S games are engineered around ultra-fast internal NVMe storage. A regular USB external drive (~100–200 MB/s) can only cold-store them — every time you want to play, you must copy the 210GB+ install back to internal storage, which takes ~30 minutes on a typical USB HDD. To actually play, you need a PS5-compatible M.2 NVMe SSD or the official Xbox Storage Expansion Card.

Compatibility FAQ

Questions Before Launch Night

Does installing an M.2 SSD void my PS5 warranty?

No. Sony designed the PS5's M.2 expansion bay to be user-accessible and publishes an official step-by-step install guide. Installing a compatible drive the way Sony describes doesn't void your console warranty — just note that physical damage caused during installation isn't covered, so power off, unplug, and follow the guide (it's one screw on PS5 Slim, plus the side panel on the original PS5). Xbox Expansion Cards simply plug into the rear port — no opening required.

Can I run the game off an external USB drive?

Not for PS5 or Xbox Series X|S titles. Next-gen games must run from internal storage, a PS5 M.2 SSD, or an official Xbox Storage Expansion Card. A USB drive can store the game, but you'd need to copy all 210GB+ back before every session (~30 minutes on a typical HDD). USB drives are great for archiving PS4 / Xbox One games, which can still be played directly from USB.

Do I really need a heatsink on my PS5 SSD?

Yes. Sony requires a cooling structure (heatsink or heat-dissipating film) on M.2 drives used in the PS5, and the total height must stay within 11.25mm. Both our PS5 picks — the WD_BLACK SN850P and Samsung 990 PRO Heatsink edition — ship with a pre-installed, PS5-compatible heatsink. Xbox Expansion Cards are fully enclosed and need no extra cooling.
